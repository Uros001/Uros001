## Description

```
In 1814, the governor of a struggling colony on the Red River made it illegal to carry one food out of his territory. Two and a half years later, more than twenty men were dead at Frog Plain. The food was pemmican: dried, pounded bison meat sealed in rendered fat and sewn into a rawhide bag. It was a Plains Indigenous technology that the fur trade could not run without.

This documentary follows pemmican from the Cree and Assiniboine camps that made it, along the North West Company's 3,000-mile canoe road, to Miles Macdonell's order of 8 January 1814, the seizure of 479 bags on the Souris River, the killings of 19 June 1816 (the Seven Oaks Massacre in settler memory, the Victory of Frog Plain for the Métis), and the collapse of the bison herds that ended the pemmican economy. It closes with the bison's return and the Buffalo Treaty of 2014.
```
