# Asset & Licence Manifest — The Pemmican War

Every element in the final film is listed here with its origin and licence. Archival paintings and photographs
come from Smithsonian Institution Open Access (CC0) and the Library of Congress (no known restrictions); three document
pages come from a printed book of 1819 on the Internet Archive (section 1b). Nothing in the film is stock
footage or AI-generated image/video. No paid service was used.

## 1b. Archival images (Smithsonian Institution Open Access and Library of Congress)

**Smithsonian** (SAAM, Archives, NMAH, National Zoo): found in the Open Access catalogue and fetched from its public
bucket (`smithsonian-open-access`, `media/<unit>/<id>`); only items whose media are marked CC0 were used.

**Library of Congress** (Prints & Photographs Division): found with the LOC public JSON search (`tools/loc_search.py`),
the item record read first (`tools/loc_item.py`; records saved in `assets/archival/loc/item_*.json`), and only items whose
rights advisory says "No known restrictions on publication" were used. Two other bison photographs (Wichita herd 1901,
Pierre 1910) were left out because the LOC lists their rights status as "not evaluated".

Each image is credited on screen (title, date, maker, collection) while it is shown. `tools/fetch_archival.sh` and
`tools/prep_archival.py` rebuild the working copies and `assets/archival/ARCHIVAL_SOURCES.json` holds every record.
Wikimedia Commons answered with HTTP 429 (too many requests from this shared address), so nothing was taken from it.

How they are used:
- Catlin painted on the upper Missouri in 1832 among Plains nations including the Assiniboine, Plains Cree and
  Blackfoot/Kainai, the economy the film describes. His paintings illustrate the bison hunt, camps drying meat, the
  people and the fur-trade post world; captions give the place and year, so none stands in for Red River in 1814.
  The Comanche village (southern plains, 1834–35) is used only for the line about women's work, with its own caption.
- Muybridge's sheet (24 views, made in Philadelphia, 1884–86, per the NMAH record) is shown as the mounted print;
  8 consecutive views (rows 3–4) are played as a loop inside a frame, and named as his photographs.
- Hornaday photographs and the National Zoo photographs are shown as what they are (1884–87 workshop, the 1887 museum
  group, a zoo bison in 2014). None is used for the 1870s commercial hunt.
- The photograph of Red River carts (B. F. Upton, Minnesota, 1867–75) is used when the narration describes the hunt and
  the carts; its caption names Minnesota and the dates, and it does not claim to show the 1840 hunt.
- The two Harper's Weekly engravings of 1874 (Frenzeny & Tavernier) illustrate the line about the 1870s hide trade.
- "Nucleus of the buffalo herd" (album of the New York Zoological Park, 1900–1920) is used for "Bison have slowly come
  back"; the caption repeats the record's title and date only.
- The three pages of the 1819 book replace the earlier generated "prop" documents. Page 134 is the real printed text of
  the proclamation, filmed like a rostrum camera over the page; yellow highlighter marks are drawn on as each term is
  spoken, located with the scan's own OCR word boxes (`tools/doc_marks.py`), and the credit says "highlighting added".
  It is a printing of the order in a trial report, not the manuscript; the wording differs slightly between 1819
  printings (see `research/FACT_CHECK.md`, C4). The glossary page is shown with the 90 lb bag and the definition of
  pemican; the title page with the lawsuits. Original page images are fetched from archive.org (`tools/fetch_documents.sh`)
  and are not kept in git; the cropped working copies and the highlight positions (`doc_marks.json`) are.

## 3. Software used (all free / open source, run locally)

Blender 5.2.3 LTS (`bpy`), Python 3, NumPy, SciPy, OpenCV, skia-python 144, pyloudnorm, soundfile,
kokoro-onnx + onnxruntime, sherpa-onnx, FFmpeg 6.1.1 (libx264, AAC). No paid API or service was used.

## 4. Things deliberately *not* used

- **Historical documents and manuscripts.** No manuscript or document facsimile appears (the order is shown as a
  labelled paraphrase). Archival images other than the Smithsonian CC0 items above were not reachable (network
  policy) and unverified mirrors were not used.
- **AI image/video generation.** Not used. (The Higgsfield account had 0 credits, and no purchase was made.)
- **Third-party music or sound libraries** other than the CC0 VSCO samples above.

## 5. On-screen disclosures

- Every archival painting or photograph carries an on-screen credit (title, date, maker, collection) while shown.
- "Reconstruction" tags on the first appearance of 3D scenes; end credits state that 3D scenes are illustrative,
  not historical records, and that the narration is a synthetic voice.
- The order of 8 January 1814 is shown as the real printed page of 1819 (credited, "highlighting added"). No generated
  document is shown any more; the former "Generated prop document" tags and the paraphrase graphic were removed in version 4.
- Map captions mark approximate boundaries, homelands, routes and positions.
