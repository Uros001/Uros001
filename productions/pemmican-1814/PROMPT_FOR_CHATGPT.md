### B2. The film that exists now (version 1, delivered)
- Topic chosen: pemmican and the Pemmican War, Red River colony, 1814–1816 ("The Pemmican War").
- 15:00.0, 1920×1080, 30 fps, H.264 CRF 18, AAC 48 kHz stereo, −14.1 LUFS, −1.3 dBTP. Cold open, five chapters, credits.
- 108 clips in version 2: about 45% maps and motion graphics (many sit on top of live 3D shots), about 42% original Blender 3D plates, about 12% real archival images (added in version 2).
- Narration: synthetic voice (Kokoro, "af_heart"), disclosed on screen and in the description. Music: original score built from CC0 orchestral samples. Sound effects are synthesized. Mix tested with loudness measurement and a speech-recognition check (about 4% word error, formatting only).
- Also delivered: vertical Short (58.5 s), 2 thumbnails, English SRT (235 cues), 5 titles, description with sources, chapters, tags, shot plan, asset/licence manifest, fact check with source links, QA report. Nothing was uploaded to YouTube.
- No AI image or video generation was used at any point.

### B3. How the visuals are made (the pipeline)
- Blender (Python module, Cycles on CPU) renders "2.5D plates": a background layer and a foreground layer per camera. A Python compositor (OpenCV/NumPy) then adds camera moves with parallax, grass and leaf wind, cloud drift, smoke, water ripple, dust, fog, grade, film grain and vignette. Graphics, maps and credits are drawn in code.
- Real-fur bison (hair curves), leaf-card oak trees, weathered lodges, a palisaded fort, a canoe landing, rawhide bags, a storehouse, a writing desk.
- Environment limits: cloud container, 4 CPU cores, 15 GB RAM, no GPU. No After Effects, DaVinci Resolve or other editor; everything is edited by script. Roughly 3–4 hours of CPU time to render all the 3D plates, about 1.5 hours to render the final picture.

### B4. Realism problems found and fixed (so you know the failure types)
Bison looked like lumps; trees looked like noodles with sparse leaves; pale "sand" bands in grass; oxen without heads; cloud streaks; storehouse nearly black; a flat ground sheet showing as a pale lake under a bison herd; dark bands under the palisade; washed-out summer grass; render noise in out-of-focus areas; white captions unreadable over snow.

### B5. Version 2 (in progress): real archival images
Network access was restricted for most of the work (Wikimedia, Library of Congress, archive.org, stock sites were blocked), so real material came only from the Smithsonian Open Access collection (public bucket, items marked CC0). The following replaced weak 3D shots, each credited on screen:
- George Catlin paintings from the upper Missouri, 1832–39 (bison hunts, herds crossing the river, a Sioux camp with meat drying, a Cree woman and an Assiniboine woman and child, a Comanche village with women dressing robes, a canoe race, Fort Union).
- Eadweard Muybridge's 1884–86 photographs of a galloping bison (8 consecutive views animated).
- William Hornaday in his taxidermy shop and the 1887 bison group he mounted; zoo bison c. 1898; a zoo bison in 2014.
Honesty rules used: every image is captioned with what it really is and where/when it was made; Catlin's Missouri paintings are not presented as Red River 1814.
