**P08** · 1:51.4–2:25.6

> The lean meat was cut into thin sheets and dried, in the sun and wind or over a slow fire, until it was hard and brittle. Then it was pounded, often on a spread hide, until it broke down into something close to coarse meal. Fat from the same animal was rendered, and the hot fat was poured over the pounded meat and worked all the way through it. Sometimes dried fruit went in, saskatoon berries or chokecherries. The mixture was packed into a rawhide bag while it was still warm, and the bag was sewn shut.
