# Topic Selection

**Date:** 2026-10-28 · **Target runtime:** ~15:00 (owner request)

## 1. Candidate concepts

**A. Pemmican and the 1814 export ban: the food that fueled a continent, started a war, and vanished with the bison**
Central question: *How could canoe brigades cross a continent without stopping to hunt, and why did a governor make their food illegal to move?*
Escalating discoveries:
1. How Plains peoples' food technology works (drying, pounding, rendered fat, a sewn hide bag; Cree *pimîhkân*).
2. Why the fur trade could not run without it (4 lb per man per day, depots ~500 miles apart, 90-lb "pieces").
3. A colony planted across the supply line, and a famine winter.
4. 8 January 1814: the export ban.
5. Seizures (479 bags at the Souris post), escalation, and 19 June 1816 at Seven Oaks/Frog Plain. A Métis victory song, and a "massacre" in the other side's telling.
6. Merger in 1821, polar expeditions.
7. The real reason it disappeared: the near-extermination of the bison (Hornaday's 1889 count: 1,091).

**B. Acorns: why California stopped eating its most abundant food**
Leaching chemistry, granaries, and the disruption of California nations' food systems.

**C. The spiral wells of Nazca (puquios)**
Underground aqueducts still in use; spiral *ojos*; a contested Nasca-versus-colonial origin.

**D. Chuño and the Inca storehouses**
Freeze-dried potatoes and hillside *qollqa* storage at altitude.

### Reasoning
- **A** combines the proven mass interest in pemmican (17M+ views across big-channel videos) with an angle almost nobody has executed well: mechanism → logistics → a *true* ban → war → disappearance. It gives the channel's old "BANNED" click power an honest, documented form. It also extends the brand from the U.S. Southwest to the Plains and fur-trade North, which is new for subscribers and broadens the audience (U.S., Canada, UK). The story has named people, dates, a turning point and a moving answer to "why did it vanish?"
- **B** has strong demand but is crowded right now: at least six copycat "why don't we eat acorns" uploads appeared in the last two weeks. Its climax is a slow structural process, not a turning point.
- **C** is gorgeous for 3D but has low proven demand and an AI-content glut; the origin debate is interesting but specialist.
- **D** is over-supplied with low-traction AI uploads, and its angle is not ours to own.

**Risks for A and their mitigations**
- *Sensitivity:* Seven Oaks is remembered differently by Métis people (the Victory of Frog Plain, *la Victoire de la Grenouillère*) and by the settler/HBC tradition (the "Seven Oaks Massacre"). The film presents both framings, names Métis, Cree, Assiniboine and other Plains peoples specifically, and does not reduce Indigenous peoples to a backdrop for a company war.
- *"Banned" accuracy:* the proclamation prohibited **export** of provisions from Assiniboia for one year without a licence. The film states this precisely in the first minute, and the title's "banned" is immediately qualified on screen.
- *No archival imagery available in this environment:* the film is built from original Blender reconstructions, real geographic data and typeset documents, all labelled as reconstructions.

## 3. Decision

**Selected: A — the 1814 pemmican export ban.**
Working film title: **"The Survival Food That Was Banned in 1814 — and Started a War"**

## 4. Title options

Title rules applied: the curiosity sits in a paradox (a survival food → banned → war); every claim is literally true (prohibited from export; the conflict is known as the Pemmican War); no "they don't want you to know".

## 5. Thumbnail concepts

**Primary — "Illegal cargo"**
- Hero object: a single sewn rawhide pemmican bag (the 90-lb *taureau*). Low-angle, close, on rough wet dock planks at dawn, with river mist behind. Rendered in Blender as an original illustration, not a photograph.
- Secondary element: a single handwritten-style order sheet nailed to a post beside it, partially legible "PROCLAMATION… 1814", with a red wax seal.
- Text (left third, 2 words, heavy condensed caps, warm off-white with a subtle shadow): **ILLEGAL CARGO**
- It complements the title (which says "survival food" and "banned") without repeating it. The image asks: *why would a bag of food be contraband?*
- Grade: muted earth tones, cold blue morning against a warm rim light on the hide. High readability at 160 px wide.

**Alternative — "What's inside"**
- The same bag in a cutaway: one side peeled back to reveal the dense meat-and-fat interior (macro). Beside it, a thin red route line on a dark river map heading toward a small fort icon.
- Text: **LASTED YEARS**
- Pairs with title #5 or #3.

## 6. Production approach (summary)

- **Runtime:** ~15:00. About 2,100–2,200 narration words at the measured ~160 wpm, plus music beats and deliberate silences.
- **Narration:** Kokoro-82M (Apache-2.0), voice `af_heart`, its highest-graded voice. Generated per paragraph, checked with Whisper speech recognition for intelligibility and pronunciation, then processed (EQ, compression, de-essing, loudness). Disclosed as synthetic.
- **Visual hierarchy, given the network limits** (Wikimedia, LOC, NARA, archive.org and YouTube are blocked here):
  1. Real geographic data: Natural Earth rivers, lakes, coasts and boundaries via GitHub for animated route and battle maps.
  2. Typeset documents with labelled re-typeset proclamation text.
  3. **Original Blender 5.2 reconstructions:** the pemmican bag (hero), a pemmican cross-section, a canoe and cargo on a misty river, Red River forks with a palisade in winter, a prairie with drying racks, and the Frog Plain at dusk.
  4. Evidence-based motion graphics: ration and depot logistics, the drying ratio, the bison population collapse.
  - **No AI-generated imagery** (no credits, and the brand needs a distinct look).
- **Sound:** an original score rendered from CC0 orchestral samples, synthesized ambiences and Foley, ducking under narration, mastered to ~-14 LUFS.
- **Deliverables:** 1080p30 H.264 MP4, SRT subtitles, thumbnails, metadata, chapters, project sources, and a vertical Short.
