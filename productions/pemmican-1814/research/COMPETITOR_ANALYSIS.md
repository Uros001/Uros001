## 3. Outlier patterns (what actually makes viewers click)

1. **Survival paradox + "without modern X"** (inkly 6–25×). The viewer can *picture* the problem: heat, cold, no fridge, no AC. The answer is a physical mechanism. → *Strongly relevant:* "how could anyone cross a continent by canoe without stopping to hunt?"
2. **A single paradox in the title** (Painting From History 10–15×: "a crop they hoped never to eat"). The title contains its own contradiction.
3. **Forgotten *usable* food** (Forgotten Crops 124×, Silas Mercer 17×, our own channel's best video at 12×). The viewer could still make or grow it.
4. **Hidden power and control** ("It Was Control", "Big Ag Buried It"). This drives clicks, **but** it usually rests on unsupported conspiracy. Our ethical version: **a documented historical act of control** (a real proclamation, real seizures, real deaths).
5. **Big-channel demonstration formats** own "how to make pemmican". We should **not** compete on recipe; we should own *the story*.

## 4. Narrative openings (from transcripts)
- **Townsends:** opens on the *problem*: voyageurs burn 4–5,000 calories a day; a canoe man can carry only ~500 lb versus a sailor's 10–20 tons. Logistics is the hook.
- **Tasting History:** personal and conversational; history arrives in blocks between recipe steps; long sponsor read.
- **inkly:** "Imagine a food you could make today, leave in a bag for years…" Second person, then the "why don't we eat it" question at 0:45. Good hook, generic payoff (refrigeration, modern snack bars).
- **Our channel's best Era B film:** a cold open on a physical object plus a "why?" question. That works, but the caveats then drain the energy.

## 5. Editing, footage and graphics (observed or disclosed; competitor playback not available)
- **Host-led channels** (Townsends, Tasting History, Clay Hayes, Kent Rollins) rely on real, hands-on footage. We cannot and should not imitate that without a presenter.
- **fern and Fall of Civilizations** set the premium faceless bar: slow, motivated camera moves through 3D or real environments; restrained scores; sound effects that make still scenes feel alive; strong maps.
- **AI faceless channels** (Forgotten Crops, Lost American Memories, Nature Lost Vault): AI stills with zooms, stock music under everything, listicle pacing. This is exactly the "AI slop" look our audience is learning to reject. One of our own commenters already used the phrase.
- **Ancient Americas:** proof that patient, honest, map-heavy storytelling can build a 300K audience in the pre-Columbian niche.

## 6. Strategic conclusions
1. **Lead with the survival mechanism; escalate with true conflict.** The pemmican market is large but recipe-saturated. The war angle alone is weak. *Mechanism → logistics → ban → war → disappearance* is the unoccupied combination.
2. **Use a paradox title** (food that "never spoils" yet "disappeared"; a food "worth a war").
3. **Differentiate on craft:** original 3D, real-data maps, motivated camera moves and a scored soundtrack, instead of AI stills.
4. **Avoid the conspiracy register** while keeping stakes. The 1814 proclamation is the rare *true* "ban" story, so the channel can use its strongest historical hook without fabricating anything.
5. **Avoid the Andean AI glut** for now: puquios, waru waru and chuño are over-supplied with low-quality uploads and have low demand.
