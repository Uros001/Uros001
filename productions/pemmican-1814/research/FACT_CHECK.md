# Fact Check — "The Survival Food That Was Banned in 1814 — and Started a War"

**Checked:** 2026-10-28
**Method:** server-side web search across reference works, museum and historical-society pages, scholarly reviews, and Métis-authored sources. Direct page fetching is blocked in this environment, so each claim rests on search-retrieved excerpts from the sources listed. Wherever a claim rests on a single or secondary source, it is marked.

**Legend**
- ✅ **Fact:** multiple reliable sources agree.
- 🟡 **Fact, single or secondary source:** used with careful wording.
- 🔵 **Interpretation:** a scholarly or community reading, presented as such.
- ⚠️ **Contested or uncertain:** presented as contested, or avoided.
- ❌ **Rejected:** not used in the film.

## G. Claims considered and rejected

| Claim | Why rejected |
|---|---|
| "Pemmican never spoils" | False as an absolute: rancidity and moisture can ruin it. We say "for years, if kept dry". |
| "The government banned pemmican" | The ban was on **export** from Assiniboia for one year, by a colony governor under HBC/Selkirk authority, not by a national government. The film states this precisely. |
| Voyageurs paddled 60 strokes/min only after pemmican, versus 40 before (Townsends) | Single anecdotal source; unverified. |
| "Four pounds of pemmican per man per day" | The *Manitoba Pageant* 4-lb figure appears to be the total food allowance, not pemmican alone. Not used. |
| A specific calories-per-pound figure | Composition varied too much; avoided. |
| Exact Seven Oaks date for the Qu'Appelle seizure ("May 12/18") | Sources disagree; replaced by "that spring". |
| Métis infinity flag "first raised at Seven Oaks" | Tradition; the MNC dates the first flying to 1815. Not used. |
| Franklin's 1819–22 expedition supplied with NWC pemmican | Not verified in this research session. Not used. |
| "Pemmican War" as a contemporary name | It is a later historians' label; the film uses it as "what historians call the Pemmican War". |

## H. Representation notes
- Indigenous peoples are named specifically (Cree, Assiniboine, Métis) where evidence supports it. Phrases such as "Plains peoples, among them…" avoid implying an exhaustive list.
- Both memories of 19 June 1816 are presented: "massacre" in the settler tradition, "victory" at Frog Plain in Métis tradition. The film does not adjudicate who fired first.
- The bison collapse is not presented as a simple villain story. Multiple causes (long-term trade pressure, commercial hide hunting, drought, cattle disease, policy) are acknowledged in compressed form.
- The narration does not speak *for* any community. Pemmican is described as living knowledge that is still practised.
